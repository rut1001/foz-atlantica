The tour

A scroll-driven film moves through the building, from the roof terrace down into a residence and out again over the Atlantic. Its seven chapters are:

1. The arrival

A residents' roof deck with a heated infinity pool, a sun terrace and two spa baths, set forty metres back from the shoreline. All twenty-eight residences face west; there are no north-facing units and no internal courtyards.

2. The living floor

Open-plan living and dining with 9.2 metres of west-facing glazing, wide-board oak flooring and a stone fireplace. Sliding doors open the full width onto the terrace. West-facing glazing brings direct sun into the living space from midday until sunset.

3. The kitchen

Natural oak cabinetry, a honed marble island with integrated wine storage, and fully integrated appliances throughout. Bedrooms are grouped behind a single door, away from the living and entertaining space.

4. The principal suite

The principal bedroom opens onto a private terrace, with a walk-in dressing room and an en-suite bathroom facing open water. Terraces run from 12 to 140 square metres; sliding walls retract fully into the structure.

5. The principal bathroom

Full-height Estremoz marble, a freestanding stone bath set into the window line, and a walk-in rain shower behind low-iron glass. Hydronic underfloor heating and mechanical ventilation with heat recovery throughout, targeting an A2 energy certificate.

6. Residents' amenities

A residents' gym facing the sea, daytime concierge, secure underground parking with one bay per residence, climate-controlled storage and private beach access. The gym has cardio and resistance equipment, a stretch studio, and a treatment room bookable through the concierge.

7. The investment

Location and context

01

Constrained supply

Foz do Douro is bounded by protected shoreline and an established low-rise fabric. New oceanfront consents in the district are granted rarely and in small numbers, which limits the volume of comparable stock that can reach the market.

02

Year-round demand

Porto is not a seasonal market. Business travel, a large international student population and a substantial remote-working base support occupancy outside the summer months.

03

Relative pricing

Prime Porto continues to trade at a lower price per square metre than prime Lisbon, and below comparable Atlantic frontage in Spain and south-west France.
